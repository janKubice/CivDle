## About This Game

```
[h1]From one hut to a sprawl of millions[/h1]

CivDle is a 2D idle city-builder that plays itself — but rewards every minute you
put in. You found a settlement, raise your first sawmill, and before long your
governor is laying out neighbourhoods along the roads, caravans are visiting
neighbouring towns, and the number at the top rolls from thousands into millions.

[h2]Build a chain, not just a building[/h2]
Wood becomes planks, planks become furniture, ore becomes steel and steel becomes
machines. Every building has inputs, outputs and people who have to work it. Zoom
in and the city is genuinely alive: traffic on the roads, citizens with names and
wishes, seasons, and weather rolling across the landscape.

[h2]Your civilisation grows while you're gone[/h2]
The governor builds against real demand — short on housing, it builds homes; short
on planks, it raises a sawmill. Paint a zone and a district builds itself inside
it, along the roads and at the best quality you can currently afford. Whatever
happened while you were away is counted up honestly when you return.

[h2]Ascend: start over, an order of magnitude higher[/h2]
When your city hits its ceiling, you can Ascend. The world resets but permanent
upgrades stay — and the next civilisation grows many times faster. Above that sits
[b]Legacy[/b]: a deeper cut that wipes even your ascensions and pays a currency
that accelerates ascending itself. And then there's the [b]Grand Work[/b] — a
bottomless build you pour your surpluses into forever.

[h2]Big numbers you can actually see[/h2]
One large ×N sits at the top: how many times stronger your civilisation is than
when it started, with a breakdown of where that strength comes from. No guessing
whether that upgrade was worth it.

[h2]A world that reveals itself[/h2]
The map starts under fog. Send scouts, build radar, and uncover volcanoes,
geysers, canyons and foreign cities — trade with them, or fold them into your
empire, buildings and all.

[h2]A game you can just watch[/h2]
Day slides into evening and the city turns into long shadows and lit windows.
Heat shimmers above the smelters, smoke drifts from chimneys, light moves across
the water and the trees sway just enough to notice. No two huts look alike. And
when you pull the camera all the way out, the view becomes a light map — your
civilisation as a constellation across a dark landscape.

[h2]What's in CivDle[/h2]
[list]
[*]Over 90 buildings, from a hut to an orbital ring
[*]Seven eras: from wood and stone to fusion and nanotech
[*]A constellation of technologies that unfolds as you research
[*]96 achievements
[*]Districts that form on their own and grant synergy
[*]Faith and prayers with real risk — call down rain, or a meteor
[*]A chronicle, charts and a timelapse of your whole run
[*]Daily challenges and contracts
[*]A photo mode that renders the scene at full detail
[*]Full terraforming — add and remove water, reshape the land under your city
[*]Full gamepad and Steam Deck support
[*]English, Czech, German, Polish, Spanish
[/list]

[h2]Moddable down to the last number[/h2]
All game content lives in JSON — buildings, resources, research, events, biomes,
weather. The built-in mod editor lets you create your own building or resource
from inside the game, draw a sprite for it and try it immediately. Then publish
to the Workshop with one button.
```

## System requirements

### Minimum
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| OS | Windows 10 64-bit |
| Processor | Dual-core 2.4 GHz |
| Memory | 4 GB RAM |
| Graphics | GPU with OpenGL 3.0 / DirectX 11 |
| DirectX | Version 11 |
| Storage | 500 MB available space |

### Recommended
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| OS | Windows 11 64-bit |
| Processor | Quad-core 3.0 GHz |
| Memory | 8 GB RAM |
| Graphics | Dedicated GPU with 2 GB VRAM |
| Storage | 1 GB available space |

> These follow from the game being 2D MonoGame on .NET 8, with the simulation
> running on aggregates rather than individuals. **Measure on your weakest
> machine before filling these in** — on Steam you are the one vouching for them.
